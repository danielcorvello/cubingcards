Intuitive F2L

CFOP: step 2

F2L stands for First Two Layers. In CFOP, it comes right after the cross. Instead of doing the bottom corners and then the middle edges, you join each corner with its edge and insert the two at once. In intuitive F2L, you do this understanding each move, without memorizing algorithms.

It is the longest step of CFOP and the one that cuts your time the most as it improves.

The pair and the slot

With the cross done, there are four spaces left between the cross edges. Each one is a slot. Each slot takes a pair: the bottom corner and the middle edge that go there.

To know which pair goes into which slot, look at the colors. The white-red-blue corner and the red-blue edge make up the pair for the slot between the red center and the blue center.

The order of the pairs doesn't matter. Start with the one that looks easiest.

The two basic cases

Every pair ends in one of these two cases, with both pieces in the top layer. Here we call them the split pair and the connected pair. In the examples, the slot is the front right one.

  • Split pair. The corner and the edge are apart. The corner is above the slot, with white facing right. The edge is at the back, on top (UB). Do R U R': the R takes the corner to the edge and forms the pair, the U puts the pair above the slot and the R' brings the two down together. On the left side, the mirror is L' U' L.
  • Connected pair. The corner and the edge are already together on top, at the front, to the left of the slot. Do R U' R': the R lifts the slot, the U' puts the pair into the space that opened and the R' brings it down. If the pair is somewhere else, turn the top first until it reaches that position.

Do these two slowly several times and watch what happens to each piece. Understanding why the pair forms is what makes the rest intuitive.

Getting the pieces to a basic case

Most of the time, the pieces don't arrive ready. The idea is always the same: move them until you land on a split pair or a connected pair.

The main tool is hiding: turn the side face so a piece goes down into a slot, move the top and then turn the face back. The hidden piece stays put while the other one moves. Always hide in an empty slot or in the pair's own slot, so you don't break a pair that is already done.

Before solving, check three things:

  1. Both pieces are on top. If one is stuck in a slot (the right one, but flipped, or another pair's slot), take it out with three moves, like R U R'.
  2. The pieces are apart. If they are joined the wrong way, split them: hide one, turn the top and bring it back.
  3. The corner's white is on the side. If the white is facing up, see the special case below.

Now look at the top colors of the corner and the edge:

  • Different colors: make a split pair. Hide the corner, take the edge to the side opposite to where you hid it and bring the corner back. Done, it's a split pair.
  • Same colors: make a connected pair. Hide the corner, with the white still on the side, bring the edge to the position where it will touch the corner and bring it back. Done, it's a connected pair.
  • White facing up (special case). Turn the top until the side color of the edge lines up with the center of that color. Hide the edge, take the corner above its place and bring it back. The pair forms and you just insert it.

When in doubt, think: where does the corner need to be for the pair to form? Take the corner there without moving the edge, or the other way around.

Use the empty slots

While there are still empty slots, they are free space. You can raise and lower a face with an empty slot without ruining anything, and that makes it easier to join the pair.

Prefer to build the pair in the slot you are solving or in an empty slot. The last pairs are the hardest precisely because there is little free space left.

An empty slot also helps when one piece of the pair is already in the right place and the other is on top. Bring the empty slot under the top piece, put it in there and return the slot. This trick is called keyhole.

Don't break the cross

Every side move you make (R, L, F, B) takes bottom pieces out of place. That's why F2L algorithms always undo what they did: R goes and R' comes back, with top moves in between.

If you raised a face, lower it before moving another one. That way the cross and the finished pairs come back whole. Also avoid turning the bottom layer (D), because the cross moves out of place.

How to practice

  • Lots of solves, no timer. At first, you will be slower than before F2L. That's normal. Without the clock running, it's easier to think about each pair.
  • Slowly. Solve F2L much more slowly than you can. The goal now is not to stop between one pair and the next.
  • One fixed way for each case. When you find a good way to solve a case, always use the same one. With time, you do the moves without looking at the two pieces.
  • Lookahead. While inserting a pair, look for the pieces of the next one. First just find where they are. Then try to follow how they move during the insert.
  • Fewer rotations. Turn the whole cube only when you need to. Inserting with the left hand (L' U' L) and using the back slots avoid many rotations. The back slots also keep the front in view, which helps lookahead.
  • Easy pairs first. Start with the pairs you can solve with just R, U and L, without F or B.
  • No regrips. Do R and L' from the normal grip and turn the top with the fingers of the other hand.
  • First pair in inspection. When the cross comes easily, try to also find the first pair during the 15 seconds of inspection.

When to move on to the algorithms

Algorithmic F2L has 41 cases, in the F2L group. It doesn't replace the intuitive one: most algorithms are just the shortest way to do what you already do.

Move on to it when intuitive F2L comes without effort and you can solve any pair, even if it takes a while. Start with the cases where you always get stuck or use many moves. The others can stay intuitive, and you learn their algorithms bit by bit, comparing them with what you already do.

Terms you will hear

  • Split pair: the basic case with the corner and the edge apart, and the three-move insert that solves it (R U R' on the right, L' U' L on the left). The slot comes up, picks up the piece and goes down.
  • Connected pair: the basic case with the corner and the edge already together on top, ready to insert (R U' R' on the right, L' U L on the left).
  • Hiding: turning the side face so a piece goes down into a slot, moving the top and turning the face back. It lets you move one piece without taking the other along.
  • Insert: putting a finished pair into its slot. "Insert the pair" is the last move of every case.
  • Taking out of the slot: bringing up a piece that is stuck below, in the wrong slot or flipped.
  • Keyhole: the empty slot trick (see "Use the empty slots").
