Ortega beginner

Face, OLL and PBL (or Varasano)

A first fast method: build just the bottom face (without caring about the sides), orient the top one (Ortega OLL, 7 cases) and permute both layers at once (Ortega PBL, 6 cases).

  1. 1st face

    The white face on the bottom, with the sexy move R U R' U'. The sides may end up swapped.

    Learn the step

  2. OLL

    With white on the bottom, make the top face all yellow. Look at how many yellow stickers are already on top and where the others are, on the sides: there are 7 cases, the same shapes as the corners of the 3x3 OLL (Sune, AntiSune, H, Pi, P, T and L).

  3. PBL

    Both faces are done: the corners just need swapping until the sides match. Look at each layer: a side with both colors matching (a "pair") means an adjacent swap (Adj); no pair, a diagonal swap (Opp). A layer that is already right has a pair on all 4 sides. The 6 cases are the combinations of the two layers; if only the bottom one is wrong, turn the cube over (x2) and it goes to the top. At the end, a U or D turn finishes it.

History of the method

  • Ortega

    The method is named after Victor Ortega, who spread it (in 2001 it went up on Josef Jelinek's site), but the idea is older: it comes from a corners-first 3x3 method, published by Jeffrey Varasano in 1981, which is why it is also called the Varasano method.
