Orientation

Guimond: step 2

With ¾ of a face on the bottom, the goal is to leave the top and bottom faces with only white and yellow, mixed. The side colors don't matter, and white may be on top: separation, the next step, takes care of that.

Find the twisted bottom corner

On the bottom, 3 corners are already right. The 4th has its white or yellow facing sideways. Turn D until it is at front-right.

Now see where its white or yellow points: to the front or to the right. That's half of the case name.

Count the top corners

The other half: how many top corners already have white or yellow facing up. Three, two (adjacent or diagonal), one or none. With one or none, the pattern on the sides tells the cases apart (A, B, C).

Turn U until the top looks like the case picture and do the algorithm. There are 16 cases in all, almost all with 3 or 4 turns: see them all in Guimond Orientation.

What if all 4 are already on the bottom?

Then only the top is left: use an Ortega OLL algorithm, treating white and yellow as if they were the same color.

Cases and algorithms

Each case with its own algorithm, sometimes shorter: to look up, practice and print.
