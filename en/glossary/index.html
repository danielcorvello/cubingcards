Glossary

What each word and acronym used in the methods, the algorithms and the timer means.

MethodsSteps and setsTermsTimer and competitions

Methods

Beginner method
Most people's first method: it solves the cube layer by layer, in 8 steps, with few algorithms. It's slow, but it teaches how the pieces move.
CFOPCross, F2L, OLL, PLL
The most used method in competitions: the cross, the 4 F2L pairs, OLL (makes the top face one color) and PLL (puts the top pieces in place).
CLL and EG
Fast 2x2 methods. In CLL, with the first layer done, the top one takes a single algorithm. In EG only the first face is needed (the bottom layer can be swapped): one algorithm (CLL, EG-1 or EG-2) solves everything else.
Fridrich
Another name for CFOP, after Jessica Fridrich, who published the complete method in 1997.
Guimond
A 2x2 method: it twists the corners so white and yellow are on top and bottom (mixed), separates the two colors and finishes with Ortega's PBL.
Ortega
A 2x2 method: it builds one face, orients the top one (Ortega OLL) and permutes both layers at once (PBL).
Reduction
For 4x4 and 5x5: build the centers and pair up the edges until the cube is like a 3x3, which is solved the usual way.
Roux
Gilles Roux's method: two 1x2x3 blocks on the sides, CMLL and the last 6 edges (LSE) with the middle layer. It uses few moves.
Sarah's method
Sarah Strong's Skewb method, made of sledgehammers and hedgeslammers: the 1st layer, the top face (Intermediate, 10 cases) and the centers; or, in Advanced, the 1st layer and the rest at once.
Top-first
A layer-by-layer Pyraminx method: the tips and centers, the 1st layer and the 3 edges left (L3E). To go faster, L4E.
Varasano
Another name for Ortega, after Jeffrey Varasano, who published in 1981 the 3x3 corners-first method it comes from.
Yau
A variation of reduction on the 4x4: two opposite centers, the cross, the other centers and the edges, in that order.
ZZ
Zbigniew Zborowski's method: it orients all the edges at the start (EOLine), and F2L is done with just R, U and L, without rotating the cube.

Steps and sets

2-Look
Doing a step in two parts, with fewer algorithms. 2-Look OLL and 2-Look PLL are the path for those moving from the beginner method to CFOP.
2GLL2-Generator Last Layer
The part of ZBLL solved with just R and U: the whole last layer, with the edges already oriented, in fast algorithms.
Adj SwapAdjacent Swap
The PLLs that swap two adjacent corners (on the same side): A, F, G, J, R and T.
Anti PLL
OLL 20 (top corners already yellow and all 4 edges flipped) together with PLL: solves the whole last layer at once.
CLSCorners Last Slot
With the yellow cross already done and the last pair's edge already in its slot, inserts the corner and makes the top face all yellow at once.
CMLL
In Roux: orients and permutes the 4 top corners, without caring about the top edges or the middle layer.
COCorner Orientation
Corner orientation: twisting the top corners until the top color is all on the top face.
COLLCorners of the Last Layer
Orients and permutes the top corners at once, when the top cross is already done; after that only an EPLL is left.
CPCorner Permutation
Corner permutation: putting the top corners, already oriented, in their right places.
Cross
The 4 edges of one face (usually white) in place, lined up with the centers. The first step of the beginner method and of CFOP.
ELLEdges of the Last Layer
Solves the last layer edges when the corners are already done.
EOEdge Orientation
Edge orientation: each edge turned so that it can go to its place without using F and B. In the last layer (in 2-Look and on the Megaminx), it means flipping the top edges until the top color all faces up: the yellow cross on the 3x3, the star on the Megaminx.
EOLine
In ZZ: orients the 12 edges and puts the front and back bottom edges in place (the line).
EPEdge Permutation
Edge permutation: putting the top edges, already oriented, in their right places.
EPLLEdges PLL
The 4 PLLs that only swap edges: Ua, Ub, H and Z.
F2LFirst Two Layers
The first two layers. In CFOP, each bottom corner goes together with the middle edge above it (a pair), one pair at a time.
FRUFF ⟨R,U⟩ F'
End of F2L with the F face turned a quarter turn: the last pair goes in with just R and U, and the final F' completes F2L.
L2CLast 2 Centers
In the 5x5 reduction: builds the last 2 centers (the top and front ones).
L2ELast 2 Edges
In the 5x5 reduction: joins the pieces of the last 2 edges, finishing the reduction.
L2LLast 2 Layers
On the Skewb: after the 1st layer, solves the top layer and the centers at once (Sarah's Advanced).
L3ELast 3 Edges
On the Pyraminx: with the first layer done, solves the 3 remaining edges.
L4ELast 4 Edges
On the Pyraminx: solves the 4 edges left after building the "V" on the bottom (the centers and 2 first-layer edges).
LLLast Layer
Last layer: the top one, which is solved last.
LSLast Slot
Last slot: the last F2L pair. Groups like WV, VLS and ZBLS solve this pair while already preparing the last layer.
LSELast Six Edges
In Roux: the last 6 edges (4 on top and 2 in the middle layer), solved with just U and M.
OLLOrientation of the Last Layer
Makes the top face all one color, without caring about the position of the pieces. There are 57 cases; in 2-Look OLL, 10 cases in two parts.
OLLCPOLL + Corner Permutation
Does OLL and puts the corners in place at the same time, leaving only an EPLL.
Opp SwapOpposite Swap
The PLLs that swap two corners diagonally: E, N, V and Y.
Parity
On the 4x4 (and even cubes), situations that don't exist on the 3x3: a single flipped edge (OLL Parity) or two swapped pieces (PLL Parity). They have their own algorithms.
PBLPermutation of Both Layers
On the 2x2 (Ortega): puts the pieces of both layers in place at once.
PLLPermutation of the Last Layer
Puts the last layer pieces in place, after OLL. There are 21 cases, named after letters (T, J, Y…); in 2-Look PLL, 6 cases in two parts.
Pure OLL
An OLL that orients the last layer without moving the pieces' positions: the PLL left is the same as before the OLL.
SBLSSecond Block Last Slot
In Roux: finishes the second block (the right one) by inserting the last pair.
Star
On the Megaminx, the 5 edges of one face in place, like the 3x3 cross. It is the first step (the white star) and, in the last layer, the yellow star of EO.
SVSummer Variation
Like WV (with the yellow cross already done), but with the pair inserted with R U' R': inserts the pair and makes the top face all yellow.
VLSValk Last Slot
Like WV, but also with flipped top edges: inserts the last pair and orients the whole last layer.
WVWinter Variation
With the yellow cross already done and the last pair built on top (inserted with R U R'), inserts the pair and makes the top face all yellow, skipping OLL.
X-Cross
The cross done together with the first F2L pair, planning it all during inspection.
ZBLLZborowski-Bruchem Last Layer
Solves the whole last layer with one algorithm, when the top edges are already oriented. There are hundreds of cases.
ZBLSZB Last Slot
Inserts the last F2L pair and orients the top edges at the same time, leaving the yellow cross ready for ZBLL.
ZZLLZZ Last Layer
In ZZ: the whole last layer in one step, when the edges are oriented and already "in phase" (the opposite ones right relative to each other). It's a subset of ZBLL.

Terms

Algorithm
A sequence of moves that solves a case. Also called alg.
AUFAdjust U Face
The turn of the top face (U, U' or U2) before or after an algorithm, to line up the case or finish the cube.
Block
A set of pieces solved together; in Roux, the 1x2x3 blocks on the sides.
Case
A cube situation that has its own algorithm (the T PLL, OLL 21…). Each card is a case.
Finger tricks
Ways of turning the faces with your fingers, without changing your grip, to do the algorithms fast.
HTMHalf Turn Metric
A way of counting moves: each face turn counts as 1, including the half turn; a middle layer turn counts as 2.
Lookahead
Looking for the next piece while still solving the current one, so you don't stop between one step and the next.
Notation
The letters that describe the moves: R, U, F… Each cube has its own notation page.
Orient and permute
Orienting is turning the piece the right way; permuting is putting the piece in the right place. OLL orients, PLL permutes.
Pair
A corner and an edge that go together into the same slot in F2L.
Pi and Peanut
On the Skewb, the names of the top-face cases in Sarah's Advanced, after the shape the yellow makes. They name the subgroups.
Recognition
Knowing which case it is by looking at the cube. In PLL, you can recognize it looking at only two faces.
Rotation
Turning the whole cube (x, y, z), without moving the pieces relative to each other.
Scramble
A sequence of moves that mixes up the cube before solving. In competitions, everyone gets the same one.
Setup
Moves made before an algorithm to bring the pieces to its position, and undone afterwards.
Sexy move
The trigger R U R' U', the most common of all.
Sledgehammer and hedgeslammer
Two triggers: the sledgehammer is R' F R F' and the hedgeslammer, F R' F' R. On the Skewb, they make up almost every algorithm in Sarah's method.
Slot
One of the 4 spaces in the first two layers where an F2L pair goes.
STMSlice Turn Metric
Another way of counting moves: each face or middle layer turn counts as 1.
Sune and Anti-Sune
Two short OLL algorithms that orient 3 corners: R U R' U R U2 R' (Sune) and R U2 R' U' R U' R' (Anti-Sune).
Trigger
A short sequence that shows up in many algorithms (R U R' U', Sledge…). Memorizing the triggers makes long algorithms easier.
Wide move
Turning a face together with the inner layers next to it: Rw (or r) turns the 2 right layers; on the 4x4 and 5x5, 3Rw turns 3.

Timer and competitions

+2
A 2-second penalty added to the time, for example for going over the 15 seconds of inspection.
Ao12Average of 12
Average of 12: drops the best and worst times and averages the remaining 10.
Ao5Average of 5
Average of 5: drops the best and worst times and averages the 3 in the middle. It's the format of 3x3 competitions.
DNFDid Not Finish
An attempt that doesn't count: the cube didn't end up solved or there was an infraction.
DNSDid Not Start
The attempt wasn't made.
Inspection
The 15 seconds to look at the cube before you start solving, without turning the faces.
Mo3Mean of 3
Plain mean of 3 times, without dropping any.
PBPersonal Best
Your personal best time, single or average.
Single
The time of a single attempt.
Stackmat
The competition timer: it starts when you take both hands off the sensor and stops when you put them back.
Sub-X
Solving under X seconds on average: sub-30, sub-20, sub-10…
TPSTurns Per Second
Turns per second: measures how fast your hands are.
WCAWorld Cube Association
The association that runs the official competitions and keeps the records.
